A. Conceptual Questions¶

$X_1$ = GPA

$X_2$ = IQ

$X_3$ = Level (1 = College, 0 = High School)

$X_4$ = GPA×IQ

$X_5$ = GPA×Level.

$\hat{\beta}_0 = 50$

$\hat{\beta}_1 = 20$

$\hat{\beta}_2 = 0.07$

$\hat{\beta}_3 = 35$

$\hat{\beta}_4 = 0.01$

$\hat{\beta}_5 = -10$.

$$\text{Salary} = 50 + 20\,X_1 + 0.07\,X_2 + 35\,X_3 + 0.01\,X_4 - 10\,X_5$$

Which answer is correct, and why?¶

iii For a fixed value of IQ and GPA, high school graduates earn more, on average, than college graduates provided that the GPA is high enoug

For fixed GPA and IQ, the only terms that change with Level are $\hat{\beta}_3 X_3 + \hat{\beta}_5 (X_1 \cdot X_3)$ College(lvl = 1): extra amount = $35(1) - 10(\text{GPA})(1) = 35 - 10\,\text{GPA}$ High Shool(lvl = 0): extra amount = $35(0) - 10(\text{GPA})(0) = 0$

So the difference (College − high School) is:

$$\Delta = 35 - 10\,\text{GPA}$$

$\text{GPA} < 3.5$ than $\Delta > 0$ than college graduates earn more $\text{GPA} > 3.5$ than $\Delta < 0$ than high school graduates earn more

Hence high school graduates earn more on average than college graduates only when GPA is high enough (> 3.5)

Predict the salary of a college graduate with IQ of 110 and a GPA of 4.0.¶

$X_1 = 4$

$X_2 = 110$

$X_3 = 1$

$X_4 = 440$

$X_5 = 4$.

$$\text{Salary} = 50 + 20(4) + 0.07(110) + 35(1) + 0.01(440) - 10(4)= 137.1 $$

than it wouldbe $137,100

True or false: Since the coefficient for the GPA/IQ interaction term is very small, there is very little evidence of an interaction effect. Justify your answer¶

False

Size of a coefficient do not tell us whether there is “evidence” of an effect by itself. Evidence comes from statistical significance which depends on estimated coefficient, standard error and sample size

A small coefficient can still be precisely estimatedand be highly significant and large coefficient can be imprecisely estimated and non-significant. So very small coefficient isnt equal very small evidence of an interaction One must check the standard error and $p$-value

def format_conf_int(ci):
    df = ci.copy()
    df.columns = ["2.5 %", "97.5 %"]
    df.index = df.index.map(lambda x: "(Intercept)" if x == "const" else x)

    print(df.to_string())

Age becomes nonsignificant once lstat and rm are included, so its effect is largely captured by the other two or is redundant.

Full model : R^2 = 0.74. Several variables (crim, zn, chas, nox, rm, dis, rad, tax, ptratio, black, lstat) are significant. indus and age add little, likely due to overlap with other predictors. The correlation heatmap confirms strong correlations among predictors, which can inflate standard errors and make some coefficients less stable.

Prediction intervals: For the 3-predictor model, the grid of lstat, rm, age shows that predicted medv increases with more rooms and decreases with higher lstat. The 95% prediction intervals give plausible ranges for a new house at each combination; they are wider than confidence intervals because they account for individual observation uncertainty.
